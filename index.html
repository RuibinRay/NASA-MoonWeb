    The creator

    Ruibin (Ray) Jin Yao

    Born in Costa Rica with Chinese roots, and now studying in Boston. Ray is completing a Bachelor of Business Administration at Hult International Business School, double majoring in Finance and Business Analytics, and speaks Mandarin, Spanish and English.

    His portfolio lists his interests as finance, business analytics, energy and technology, international business, and entrepreneurship. He has worked in international business development for a company that makes long-duration energy storage, which is the same problem a Moon base faces every two-week night.

    MOONKEEPER is his entry for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, made as team Nasa Neo: a game that teaches how people could live beyond Earth by letting players solve the problems themselves.

    How the project came together

      SI-generated

      The Moon · Sea of Tranquility

      The Moon is beautiful.
      Surviving it is not.

      A meteor shower has struck every station on the Moon. You are the only one left at Tranquility Deep, a base inside a real lava-tube pit. Keep it running until the rescue ship lands.

      Scene A · The impact

      It starts with a strike

      With no air to burn them up, even small meteoroids reach the Moon's ground at 20 kilometres a second or more. In the game every strike and every moonquake is taken from records made by the seismometers the Apollo crews left behind: 213 impacts and 28 shallow moonquakes.

      Real data: Apollo seismic catalogStory: a shower that hits every station

      Scene B · Survival

      Power on, air in

      Every room is a sealed bubble of air behind a bulkhead door. When one is holed you patch it, or lock its door to save the rest of the station and rebuild that room later. Power, oxygen, water and food all run down unless you act, and no choice is free: every machine eats power, every helper eats something, every fix costs time.

      Real: sealed sections, as on real space stationsSimplified: repair times

      Scene C · Engineering

      One station, eleven rooms

      Choose a room in the cross-section. One Power Board runs the whole base: turning the Oxygen Maker up leaves less for the farm lights or the heaters.

      Scene D · Real-world skills

      Every job is done by hand

      Before each big job there is a short hands-on game based on a real procedure. There are sixteen:

        Simplified: each takes seconds, not hours

        Scene E · The lunar night

        Two weeks without the Sun

        A day on the Moon lasts about 29.5 Earth days, so night lasts about two weeks. Solar panels make nothing, the ground falls to around −130 °C, and the base must live on its battery and a small reactor. Drag the slider to see why the game makes you fill the battery and the oxygen tank before sunset.

        Real: the length of the lunar daySimplified: this diagram's battery numbers

        Mars · Arcadia Planitia

        The next frontier is red.

        The cheap way to Mars opens about once every 26 months, and the trip takes around nine months. The game's second world is an outpost on a cold plain with ice under the soil.

        Playable now, and still growing. Mars is in the game as an outpost you can explore and a first survival mission. Flights between the Moon and Mars are planned, not built.

        What is MOONKEEPER?

        More than a game.

        A free 3D game for ages 7 to 18 that runs in a browser, with no download and no login. It mixes survival, engineering decisions, resource management and exploration, and explains each idea at the moment the player needs it.

                Life-support lab

                Oxygen from water

                Electricity splits water into hydrogen and oxygen: 2 H₂O → 2 H₂ + O₂. Nine kilograms of water hold eight kilograms of oxygen. The International Space Station makes part of its oxygen this way.

                Real: the chemistry, and 0.84 kg a day per personSimplified: the game's machine sizes
